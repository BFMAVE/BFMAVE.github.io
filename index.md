# Brecht F. Verbeken[*](https://bfmave.github.io/#name-note)

I am a quantitative economist (PhD) and mathematician (MSc) by training, and I think of myself as a data scientist. My interests centre on economics and AI, with a lasting passion for mathematics.

I particularly enjoy bringing people with different specialities together and the cross-fertilisation of ideas that comes from interdisciplinary work. I often help set the research agenda and lead collaborations, as I have done since the early years of my PhD.

I am a postdoctoral researcher and guest professor at the [Vrije Universiteit Brussel](https://researchportal.vub.be/en/persons/brecht-verbeken/). More recently, I have become particularly interested in the economic impact of AI, especially how it changes scientific discovery and innovation. My work spans economic forecasting, model evaluation, and AI in mathematics and science.

I am always curious about my colleagues’ research. My quantitative background often gives me a way to contribute, and these collaborations have taken me into many different areas. Sometimes this leads to an acknowledgement; sometimes to co-authorship. Perhaps the common thread is helping others move their research forward.

I am proud of the team and colleagues I work with, at the [Data Analytics Lab](https://data.research.vub.be/people)  and beyond. Much of this work reflects our shared effort.

## Current research

### Economics

Regarding my interests and work:

“An economist who is only an economist cannot even be a good economist.”
[F. A. Hayek, interview with Leo Rosten (1978)](https://static.library.ucla.edu/oralhistory/text/masters/21198-zz0008zd21-4-master.html)

More recently, my interests have turned to the economic effects of AI, particularly its effects on scientific discovery and innovation. This includes how AI changes the pace and direction of research, which questions receive attention, and how verification costs affect the value of automation.

Colleagues at VUB, led by Andres Algaba, are exploring this line of work through [*The Rate and Direction of Automated Science*](https://press.vub.ac.be/onderzoeksgroep-van-vub-geselecteerd-voor-internationaal-onderzoeksprogramma-van-openai), a project selected for OpenAI’s Economic Research Exchange.

Related work: [LLM-generated research methods](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2606.26130)  and [scientific literature and citation practices](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2504.02767).

My economics work also includes [economic forecasting](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-nowcasting), decision-making under uncertainty, [public investment and transport accessibility](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2509.22223), and [ergodicity economics](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-ergodicity)  with [Arne Vanhoyweghen](https://data.research.vub.be/arne-vanhoyweghen0)  and colleagues.

I have also served as an examiner, supervisor or co-supervisor for master’s theses spanning a wide range of economics and business topics.

## Mathematics

Mathematics is a lasting passion. My main mathematical research line concerns Markov chains and stochastic matrices, particularly their eigenvalues and spectral regions. It also connects to my work on semi-Markov models and applications in manpower planning.

I occasionally return to algebra, closer to my [master’s thesis on units in integral group rings](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-masters-thesis): [group rings](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2608.03254), the [Prime Graph Question](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2607.29105), and [braces](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2608.01884). More recently, a [paper on Ramsey graphs](https://bfmave.github.io/research.html#paper-2610.06081)  has provided another setting in which to explore AI-assisted mathematical research.

A personal project close to my heart is an [illustrated exploration of the Karpelevič theorem](https://bfmave.github.io/karpelevic/), developed with Vincent Ginis. It brings together a proof, geometric explanations and numerical illustrations of the possible eigenvalues of stochastic matrices. I see this kind of exposition as a service to the mathematical community—work that, in my view, is too often undervalued in science.
