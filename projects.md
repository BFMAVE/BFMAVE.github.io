## AI and scientific discovery

Research interests · Economics of AI and innovation

More recently, I have become interested in the economic impact of AI on scientific discovery and innovation: which parts of research become faster, which questions receive attention, and how verification costs affect the value of automation.

Colleagues at VUB, led by Andres Algaba, are exploring this line of work through The Rate and Direction of Automated Science, a project selected for OpenAI’s Economic Research Exchange.

[The team’s automated-science project](https://press.vub.ac.be/onderzoeksgroep-van-vub-geselecteerd-voor-internationaal-onderzoeksprogramma-van-openai) [Related working paper: LLM-generated research methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26130)

## Evaluating AI-supported science for policy

VUB collaboration with the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre (JRC)

AI-supported research tools can produce useful policy-facing material, but their outputs need to be assessed for scientific accuracy, traceability and critical errors. Through a VUB collaboration with the JRC, I was involved in developing evaluation templates and contributed to a validation protocol for AI-supported research outputs.

The work covers scientific accuracy, source traceability, critical failures, reviewer agreement and workflow effort. It connects my interests in model evaluation and human validation with the practical demands of research used in public policy.

[JRC’s work on AI and data (institutional context)](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/what-we-do/scientific-portfolios/ai-and-data_en)

## Public-sector AI review: FARI / AI4Gov-X

FARI · AI4Gov-X Accelerate · Public-sector AI and digital governance

For FARI within AI4Gov-X Accelerate, I co-authored an internal review of AI4Scale requirements and prototype validation. The review addressed feasibility, prioritisation, deployment, governance and process ownership.

AI4Gov-X is co-funded by the European Union’s Digital Europe Programme and supports skills, experimentation and digital governance in the public sector. FARI, the joint VUB–ULB AI for the Common Good Institute, leads work on the AI4Scale platform and the AI4Engine framework.

## Course information sheets and institutional AI adoption

Vrije Universiteit Brussel · Academic quality assurance

Higher education institutions need to understand how generative AI affects course design and assessment. I led the technical implementation of a reusable LLM-assisted workflow for classifying course information sheets at VUB.

The initial production scan covered 4,684 course information sheets. The approach combines a pilot phase, prompt development and model comparison, human validation, automated reporting and a later repeat scan. The emphasis is on a method that an institution can inspect, validate and reuse.

[Working paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13562) [Paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13562)

## Brussels Metro 3 under uncertainty

Public investment · Transport accessibility · Distributional analysis

Public investment decisions require more than an average effect. I led a study comparing transport accessibility under current, partial and full Metro 3 scenarios, using official public-transport schedules and open-map data.

The analysis examines geographical differences and sensitivity to departure timing. The work connects quantitative modelling with questions about who benefits from infrastructure investment and how uncertainty should enter public debate.

[Working paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22223) [Paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22223) [BRUZZ coverage](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/mobiliteit/onderzoekers-over-metro-3-amper-voordelen-bij-halve-aanleg-2025-10-03)
